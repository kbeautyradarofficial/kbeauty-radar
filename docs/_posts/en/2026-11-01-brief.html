1. October exports +58%, and 2025's full-year total is already beaten

Korea exported $1.44B of cosmetics in October 2026, up 58% from a year earlier. CNC News says that is the fastest growth in three years. The figure comes from the trade ministry's monthly trade report. (CNC News)

  • Exports for January–October 2026 totalled $12.57B. That is about $1.1B more than all of 2025 ($11.4B).
  • Exports to the US were $230M in October (+62.7%). The US was still the largest single destination that month.
  • Cosmorning reports a full-year 2026 projection of up to $15.255B. (Cosmorning)

For buyers: Demand for Korean supply is growing in every region, so Q4 reorders will compete for production slots. Confirm Q1 2027 purchase orders and MOQs with your suppliers now rather than after the holidays.

Reading the numbers: Korea publishes three export series, and each one counts differently. The trade ministry's monthly report gives $12.57B for Jan–Oct. The food and drug ministry (MFDS) reported $11.1B for Jan–Sep (+31.1%), according to MBC News. Customs releases preliminary data every 10 days. When a supplier quotes a growth figure, ask which series and which period it comes from.

2. EU +106% in October: Europe is now almost as big as China

Cosmetics exports to the EU more than doubled in October, rising 106.1% to $130M. Exports to China grew 23.4% to $140M. Exports to the CIS rose 60.5% to $80M, and exports to the Middle East rose 29.5% to $40M. (CNC News)

  • So far in 2026, 22 countries have bought more than $100M of Korean cosmetics, up from 17 the year before.
  • In Q3 cumulative terms, growth was highest in the Netherlands (+294%), Estonia (+213%), the UK (+179%) and Canada (+122%).

The Netherlands and Estonia serve as distribution hubs, so their growth suggests product is flowing through them into the wider EU. Europe is no longer an afterthought for Korean brands.

For EU distributors and retailers: Before you list a product, confirm that each SKU has a CPNP notification and a named Responsible Person. Brands that rush into Europe are the ones most likely to have gaps.

3. Amorepacific Q3: operating profit +25%, net income -14%

Amorepacific's Q3 2026 consolidated revenue was KRW 1.1295T (+11.1%), and operating profit was KRW 115.2B (+25.4%). These are preliminary figures disclosed on October 29. Net income fell 14.3% to KRW 58.5B, and pre-tax profit fell 28.1% to KRW 71.0B. (SatEconomy)

  • For Q1–Q3, revenue was KRW 3.4411T (+11.4%) and operating profit was KRW 359.3B (+26.8%).
  • SK Securities reportedly forecast KRW 108.4B of Q3 operating profit and about 24% North America revenue growth. The actual operating profit beat that forecast. (SatEconomy)

The core business did well. Because operating profit rose while pre-tax profit fell, the drop came from non-operating items. The question for Q4 is how much Western-market margin is left after Amazon and Black Friday promotion spending.

For retailers: Large Korean brands are likely to push hard on promotions this quarter. Agree on co-op funding and price floors before the event calendar fills up.

4. Pharma meets fashion: Ildong's microbiome beauty joint venture

Dailypharm reports that Ildong Pharmaceutical and the fashion company Nine & Company have formed a joint venture called IDNINE. The deal was announced in August 2026. The venture will cover cosmetics, inner beauty, supplements and beauty devices. Its core asset is the microbiome ingredient Cera-Glow, and derma skincare products are planned from 2027. (Dailypharm)

Manufacturing capacity is growing too. IB Tomato reports that Cosmax plans a third plant in Pyeongtaek costing about KRW 120B. Once it is built, Cosmax will have 8 plants in Korea. (IB Tomato)

For buyers: Pharma-backed derma lines built on proprietary ingredients are due in 2027. If you are planning a clinical-skincare range, start those conversations early.
