1. Korean SME cosmetics exports: $2.79bn in Q3, a record

Korean small and mid-sized cosmetics makers exported $2.79bn in Q3 2026, up 28.6% year on year. The January–September total reached $7.65bn (+26.4%). Both are records (MTN, citing the Ministry of SMEs and Startups).

The number of exporting SMEs rose only 6.3% to 9,469 in Q3 (SBS Biz). Value grew more than four times faster than the supplier count, so the growth comes from existing suppliers getting bigger reorders, not a wave of new brands. All SME exports grew 8.8% to $33.4bn in Q3 (Korea Economic Daily), so cosmetics grew roughly three times as fast as the total.

For buyers: Reorders are driving this growth, so the suppliers you already use are getting busier. Book Q1 2027 purchase orders and confirm MOQs early.

2. The US passed China as Korea's top SME export market

Korean SME exports to the US hit $5.73bn in Q3 2026 (+19.6%), ahead of China. The ministry credits wider K-beauty placement at major US retailers (Korean Center / KITA). For context, KOTRA data shows Korea was the No.1 source of US cosmetics imports in 2024, with a 22.4% share after a 54.3% jump in shipments (IB Tomato). That exposure is why the tariff question keeps coming back.

3. Cross-border online: China still leads, and online duty-free is why

Statistics Korea puts Q3 2026 cross-border online sales at ₩1.0944tn (+40.7%), the highest third quarter in six years. Cosmetics made up ₩636.3bn of that (+47.1%), about 58%. By destination: China ₩432bn (+62.3%), US ₩278.7bn (+30.4%), Japan ₩200.2bn. Online duty-free sales nearly doubled to ₩371.7bn (+90.5%), about 82% of it cosmetics. Dealsite notes that without duty-free, the US is the largest market (Youth Daily / Dealsite).

The two datasets measure different things. The ministry counts customs-cleared exports, mostly B2B. Statistics Korea counts direct-to-consumer online sales, including duty-free. Duty-free growth into China may feed daigou resale, and that stock can end up undercutting prices elsewhere.

On the export side, SME online cosmetics exports nearly doubled to $360m in Q3 (+97.5%), 75.3% of all SME online exports (Seoul Economy).

For buyers: Before you sign exclusivity or a MAP agreement, ask the brand how it controls duty-free and gray-market flows.

4. Mass retail push: The Face Shop and Goodal

LG H&H's The Face Shop was in about 1,600 US Walmart stores as of July 2026 (Newsis). Its rice-water line has sold over 49m units, and 6 SKUs launched at Target (~1,900 stores) in September 2025 (Smart Today). Newsworks reports the line is now heading to Ulta Beauty; store count and timing are unconfirmed (Newsworks). Happi reports Clio's Goodal is launching its Green Tangerine Vita C Serum at Costco; store count, price and region are unconfirmed (Happi).

Checklist when a brand sits in both mass/club and specialty retail:

  • Club-only pack sizes or bundles so shoppers can't compare prices one-to-one
  • Separate price tiers and a written MAP policy
  • A contract clause that says who absorbs tariffs: brand, importer or shelf price

In the UK, 365 Retail reports Biodance is opening its first physical counter at Selfridges London. Selfridges already sells the brand online (Sea Kelp Serum 30ml, £22 as of November 2026), but we couldn't confirm the counter (Selfridges).

5. The warning in the same release: SME car exports −42.4%

In the same release that reported record cosmetics numbers, Q3 2026 SME car exports fell 42.4% to $1.5bn because the Middle East war blocked shipping routes (Korean Center). Cosmetics escaped it this quarter, but they move on the same sea lanes.

For buyers: Build extra lead time into holiday and Q1 replenishment, and agree with suppliers in advance on who pays if a shipment has to switch to air freight.
