Bottom line: brands now pick the P&L before they pick the model

October's ad deals share one pattern. Brands decide which market and which channel a model has to move before they look at fame. Seoul OOH is being bought as content for overseas sales. Global ambassador campaigns get market-specific prices and bundles. Idol models are now in demand outside cosmetics.

1. 75% of sales overseas, ads in Seongsu and Hongdae

Money Today reports that overseas markets make up 75% of AGE20'S sales (reported Oct 2 with the rebrand). Japan is the main growth market, and the brand is expanding into the US, Europe, Southeast Asia, Russia/CIS and the Middle East. CosinKorea reports more than 240 million units sold across 30 countries through June 2026. The "Global No.1 pact" wording is the brand's own and is not backed by any official ranking.

Even so, the ads are in Seoul. Reports say IVE's Rei is on OOH in Gangnam, Seongsu, Hongdae and subway stations, after the Seongsu pop-up "THE AGE20'S HOUSE" opened on Oct 1 (Consumer Times). According to the Korea Herald (Herald Economy), the new core target is women aged 28–34, and the first launch is the semi-matte Skin First Hidden Prep Foundation Pact. The parent group's cosmetics business was 32% of sales in FY2025, with a goal of more than 50% by 2028. US shades now go up to 20.

How to read it: tourist districts work as social content that reaches shoppers in Japan. Judge the spend by sell-through to inbound tourists and by social reach in Japan, not by recall at home.

2. HERA x Felix: a Japan-specific price list

HERA started its global "Still Glowing" Reflection campaign with Felix of Stray Kids on Sep 28. According to Amorepacific Japan's press release, the Reflection Skin Glow Powder (8g, 01 Glow and 02 Lavender Glow) sells for ¥5,500 including tax in Japan. A limited set (foundation 30g, powder, UV tone-up and base) sells for ¥12,980. We did not find a Korean won price.

The point for buyers: one global face does not mean one global assortment. Ask for the market-specific bundle and price list, not just the key visual.

3. Idol models move into eye drops and patches

Medigate News reports that JW Pharmaceutical named aespa as the model for Friends eye drops and the iSsuk eyelid cleaner, with the campaign starting Oct 20. Friends has been No.1 in Korea's OTC artificial-tears market for 13 straight years (IQVIA, 2013–2025). Three variants were repackaged and now contain hyaluronic acid as an additive.

Microbiome derma brand EIOM named D.O. as its model on Oct 15, focusing on its Trouble Biome Patch Mask. The mask was an Olive Young "올영픽" pick in June (Wikitree). Pharmacy and derma brands now bid for the same idol models as color cosmetics brands.

4. ₩200bn without Olive Young, and now Jeon Do-yeon

Fusen Bio says CELLAB Biogenic Essence passed ₩200bn in cumulative single-product sales by the end of 2025. Going from ₩100bn to ₩200bn took 14 months, and sales came mainly through its own online mall (D2C), without Olive Young or Daiso (Daum News). Reports say a new TV commercial with actress Jeon Do-yeon launched Oct 1, following earlier work with Kim Hye-ja (ZDNet Korea). For a brand built on one hero product, the model is a lever on customer acquisition cost and repeat purchases.

What to watch next quarter

  • Inbound-tourist sell-through as the KPI for Seoul pop-ups and OOH
  • Market-specific bundles and pricing for global ambassador launches
  • Idol model fees benchmarked against OTC and derma categories
  • Repeat-purchase data from D2C hero brands before listing them
