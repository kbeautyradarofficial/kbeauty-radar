1. $11.1B in nine months, with the UK and Netherlands growing fastest

Korean cosmetics exports for January–September 2026 were a provisional $11.1B, up 31.1% year on year. That is close to the full-year 2025 total of $11.4B. These are MFDS figures, not Korea Customs Service data. (CEO Score Daily)

  • US: $2.35B, or 21.1% of all cosmetics exports (Jan–Sep)
  • UK: +179.3% YoY; Netherlands: +292.6% YoY (Jan–Sep)

The US brings in more money. Europe is growing much faster. Industry coverage says Boots now carries more than 40 K-category brands and that K-beauty sales there grew more than fivefold over the past year, though it gives no reference period. (Cosmetics Business coverage)

For buyers: More Korean brands are pitching UK and EU shelves. Before you commit space, confirm that each brand's CPNP/UKCA notification and PPWR packaging plan are done. Unregistered SKUs will hold up your first delivery.

2. Kolmar's operating profit rose 41.8%; Cosmax's rose 13.0%

In H1 2026, Kolmar Korea's revenue was KRW 1.5893T (+14.8%) and its operating profit was KRW 189.2B (+41.8%). Cosmax's revenue was KRW 1.4769T (+21.8%) and its operating profit was KRW 126.8B (+13.0%). Cosmax set quarterly records for both revenue and operating profit in Q2. (CEO Score Daily)

One report puts Kolmar Korea's Q3 revenue at KRW 828.2B (+21.3%) and operating profit at KRW 81.9B (+40.5%). We could not find the original article. (report)

Our read: the export boom is not lifting every manufacturer's margin the same way. Kolmar's profit grew about three times faster than its revenue. Cosmax's profit grew more slowly than its revenue. When an ODM's margins are widening, brands have less room to push back on price, and that cost can end up in wholesale prices.

For buyers: Ask brand partners which ODM makes each hero SKU. Lock in 2027 pricing and MOQ on those SKUs early.

3. Pharma Research: Q3 cosmetics exports +92%

Pharma Research's provisional Q3 2026 consolidated revenue was KRW 171.5B (about +27%). Operating profit was KRW 62.6B, a 36.5% margin. Overseas sales were about 48% of revenue. Cosmetics exports were KRW 42.1B (+92%), domestic cosmetics sales were KRW 19.9B (+50%), and medical-device exports were about KRW 30B (+53%). (Insight)

Another report on the same provisional filing says operating profit grew only 1.2% year on year. That is far below revenue growth, so wait for the final results before relying on either number. (Smart Today)

For buyers: At this company, cosmetics exports (+92%) grew faster than domestic cosmetics sales (+50%). If your derma assortment is thin, put the category on the list for 2027 line reviews.

4. Olive Young plans seven US stores; 27,000 shoppers used Skin Scan

Olive Young plans five more California stores by H1 2027, which would bring its US total to seven. The new sites include Fashion Valley in San Diego, its first store outside the LA area. Its two current stores are Pasadena (opened May 2026) and Westfield Century City (opened June 2026). Simon Property Group operates the Fashion Valley, Del Amo and Brea Mall locations. (Korea JoongAng Daily)

Olive Young has not disclosed US store sales. About 27,000 shoppers used its in-store Skin Scan service between May 29 and the end of October.

For US retailers: Olive Young is now a physical competitor that also collects first-party skin data. Track which brands it launches first in the new stores. Those launches will show which brands it plans to push hardest with US shoppers.

5. Anua took a Seoul pop-up to two LA Ulta stores 11 days later

Reports say Anua ran a KPop Demon Hunters "Hunters Beauty House" pop-up at Olive Young N Seongsu on October 1–12, then held truck events at two LA Ulta stores on October 23–24. We found no sales figures. (ContentGrip)

For retailers: Ask Korean brand partners about their IP and entertainment tie-ins before the plans are final. An event outside your store can bring in traffic that a shelf placement alone won't.
