1. October exports: $1.44B, up 58%. Jan–Oct already tops all of 2025

Korea's Ministry of Trade puts October cosmetics exports at $1.44B, up 58% year on year and the fastest growth in three years. The Jan–Oct total of $12.57B already beats the full-year 2025 figure of $11.4B. (CNC News)

October by region: US $230M (+62.7%), EU $130M (+106.1%), China $140M (+23.4%).

  • For buyers: the US is the volume and the EU is the speed. Lock Q1 2027 allocations and MOQs with Korean suppliers now, before holiday reorders tie up capacity.
  • EU distributors should confirm CPNP notifications and Responsible Person arrangements for every new SKU.

2. China: five months of growth, but Korean brands hold just 4% of the market

October was the fifth straight month of growth in exports to China since they turned positive in June (CNC News). Even so, the Jan–Oct total was $1.77B, up only 2.7%. That is less than half the 2021 peak of $4.88B. The article does not name the agency behind this figure. (Seoul Economic Daily)

The market has moved. In 2025, local Chinese brands held 57.4%, up from 52.8% in 2023. Korean brands held 4%, behind France (12%), the US (11.74%) and Japan (6.39%). Per Euromonitor, K-skincare brands in China's top-300 e-commerce beauty ranking fell from 19 in 2022 to 11 in 2025, and K-color brands fell from 25 to 14. (Seoul Economic Daily)

The brands going back in are taking new routes. APR's Medicube entered about 1,500 beauty stores, including WOW COLOUR and Harmay, through a local distributor. The Founders' Anua joined Sephora China in August. Born to Standout is partnering with a local group on a Shanghai flagship next year. (Seoul Economic Daily)

  • For partners in China: expect K-brands to pitch offline beauty-store and prestige retail placement, not just cross-border e-commerce. Wide distributor-led placement and single-retailer entry carry very different margin and inventory risk.

3. Olive Young's first color-only store: 26% of brands are new to the chain

Olive Young opens "Color Ground Hongdae" in Seogyo-dong, Seoul, on November 20. The store has four floors and 575㎡, and it is the chain's first store built around a single category. (Financial News)

From January to October 2026, foreign shoppers made 84% of sales at Olive Young's Hongdae-area stores, and customers in their teens and 20s were more than half (Financial News). Olive Young says its color cosmetics sales grew 36% a year on average over the past five years. This store brings its Hongdae count to 10. (Herald Economy)

26% of the color brands in the store are new to Olive Young, grouped in a third-floor "Rookie Zone". Shelves are sorted by product type, texture and function, not by brand. (Insight)

  • For buyers: the Rookie Zone is a free scouting list of next-wave indie color brands, shown to a mostly international crowd. Put it on your next Seoul trip.

4. No Brand derma skincare at ₩9,980, rolling into E-mart

No Brand launched six derma skincare SKUs made with Onebiogen in late October, at ₩9,980 each: a cleanser, toner, mist, serum and two creams. They sold first in No Brand stores and expand to E-mart in November. (NewsWhoPlus)

Test sales reportedly passed 1,000 units (Green Economy). E-mart's beauty range priced under ₩5,000 has reportedly grown to 85 items, with skincare sales up about 12% in Jan–Nov (iNews24). A five-SKU PDRN line is reportedly planned for November, plus 12 hair and body items with Kundal in December. (Korea Report)

  • For private-label buyers: trend ingredients like PDRN are reaching sub-₩10,000 supermarket PB in Korea. Use this tier to benchmark your own PB derma pricing and supplier quotes.
