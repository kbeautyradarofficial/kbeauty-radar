Bottom line: Korean cosmetics exports covered 98% of last year's total in nine months. Growth came from higher skincare prices, the US and a fast-rising Europe. The Q4 risk is not demand. It is ODM capacity, tube packaging and currency.

1. September: the first $1.5B month

  • September exports were $1.51B, up 31.4% YoY, per the trade ministry's monthly data. The previous monthly record was $1.35B in April 2026. That makes 11 straight months of growth. (CNC News)
  • On the same data, Jan–Sep exports were $11.14B (+30.8%), 98% of the 2025 full-year total of $11.42B.
  • MFDS's provisional Jan–Sep figure is $11.1B (+31.1%). Q3 alone was $4.17B, up 7.5% from Q2's $3.88B. (Medipharm Health News)

2. Supply: top ODMs booked to year-end, tubes short

  • Hana Securities (Oct 14 report) named a weaker dollar and packaging shortages as the two swing factors in Q3. (Pinpoint News)
  • Orders for tube formats such as sunscreen surged. Some orders at d'Alba Global, Cosmecca Korea and Kolmar Korea were pushed into Q4. Tubes are heat-sensitive, so quality concerns make Chinese substitutes hard to use. Top ODMs including Cosmax and Kolmar are reportedly full through year-end and struggling to take new clients. (Mega Economy)
  • Shinyoung Securities expects the issue to ease as brands add packaging suppliers. (S-Journal)
  • Capacity is coming. Kolmar Korea is raising capacity from 370M to more than 630M units. Cosmax is investing over KRW 260B to reach 4B units a year. Cosmecca Korea approved KRW 136B for a skincare plant in Ochang. The Q4 business outlook index for cosmetics makers is 120, up more than 20 points QoQ. (CNC News)
  • The won strengthened from 1,406.3 per dollar in August to 1,359.0 in September, so the same dollar sales convert into fewer won.

3. Price: volume +15.7%, value +49.4%

  • The Beauty Economy's analysis of August customs data: skincare export weight rose 15.7%, value rose 49.4%, and the average unit price rose 29.0%. Total August cosmetics exports were $1.31B (+52.1%). (The Beauty Economy)
  • August skincare prices per kg were highest for the UAE ($53.9), the Netherlands ($49.4) and the UK ($43.4), well above the US and China. Skincare unit prices to Japan rose 42.6%.
  • Jan–Sep by type (MFDS): skincare $8.72B (+45.1%, about 79% of the total), color cosmetics $1.11B (-3.6%), body cleansing $0.56B (+30.1%). Skincare to the US rose 48.8%, from $1.21B to $1.80B.

4. Markets: US 21.1%, Europe is the second engine

  • Jan–Sep (MFDS): US $2.35B (21.1% share, +40.6%), China $1.58B (14.2%, +0.4%), Japan $0.89B (8.0%, +8.6%).
  • On a Jan–Sep basis, China's share fell from 46.7% in 2022 to 14.2%. The US share rose from 10.8% to 21.1%.
  • The UK reached No. 5 for the first time, passing Russia and Vietnam. The Netherlands (8), Poland (9) and Canada (10) also entered the top 10 for the first time. Jan–Sep growth: UK +180%, Netherlands +292%, Canada +124%. (Cosinkorea, CNC News)
  • September only (trade ministry): US $260M (+21.9%), China $210M (+13.6%), EU $170M (+110.4%), Middle East $50M (+37.2%). (CNC News)

5. Regulation: EU safety assessment is shifting

  • The Korea Cosmetic Industry Institute held its K-Beauty export regulation conference on Oct 14–15. Sessions covered EU/UK, ASEAN and Australia/New Zealand rules, plus NAMs and next-generation risk assessment (NGRA). SCCS co-chair Prof. Vera Rogiers was among the speakers. (Cosinkorea, CNC News)
  • The direction: less reliance on animal data and more human-relevant science to prove safety.

What buyers and retailers should do now

  • Confirm production slots and tube supply before committing to Q1 suncare launches.
  • Budget for firmer Korean wholesale prices; skincare is selling on price power, not just volume.
  • European retailers: expect more Korean brand pitches and screen for EU-ready paperwork.
  • Ask for each SKU's safety assessment file at the start of onboarding.

Sources: CNC News, Medipharm Health News, The Beauty Economy, Pinpoint News, Mega Economy, S-Journal, Cosinkorea (citing MFDS, trade ministry and customs data, and Hana Securities and Shinyoung Securities reports).
