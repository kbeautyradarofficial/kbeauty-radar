1. Europe is growing fastest, but the US is still the biggest market

Korea's cosmetics exports for January–September 2026 were a provisional $11.1B, up 31.1% from $8.5B a year earlier. That is already about 97% of the 2025 full-year total of $11.4B (MFDS data via Youth Daily). Q3 alone was $4.17B, up 7.5% from $3.88B in Q2. Keep the quarterly figure separate from the cumulative total.

  • US: $2.35B, 21.1% share, +40.6% (Jan–Sep)
  • China: $1.58B, 14.2% share, +0.4% (flat)
  • UK: $443M, +179.3%
  • Netherlands: $369M, +292.6%
  • Poland: $322M, +58.6%
  • Canada: $285M, +124.4%

The Netherlands figure most likely reflects Rotterdam's role as an EU distribution hub, not Dutch demand alone. If so, Korean brands are supplying the wider EU through hub logistics.

Newsis reported that Korea's exports to Europe passed North America for the first time in H1 2026. However, the US alone accounts for $2.35B in Jan–Sep, so that comparison depends on how the regions are defined.

For buyers: Before you sign an exclusive deal, check that the brand already has a CPNP notification and an EU Responsible Person. Brands that have both can handle larger reorders sooner.

2. Skincare is up 45.1% while makeup is down 3.6%

In the same Jan–Sep data, skincare exports reached $8.72B (+45.1%), while makeup fell to $1.11B (−3.6%). Skincare accounts for most of K-beauty's export growth. Korean makeup is under pressure from Chinese brands.

For buyers: Put more of your K-beauty open-to-buy into skincare. When you list Korean makeup, choose SKUs that offer skincare benefits over pure color lines.

3. L'Oréal in Korea: it paid ₩600B+ for each brand it bought, and the brand it built shut down

L'Oréal paid ₩668.2B for 100% of Dr.G in 2025. Seven years earlier, in 2018, it bought 3CE for about ₩600B (Asia Economy).

Sihyo, its own Korean-inspired brand, launched in 2022 through Rosian, a joint venture with Hotel Shilla and Anchor Equity. It closed in February 2025 and left a ₩9.24B impairment. 3CE sales peaked at ₩269.5B in 2019 and were ₩224.8B in 2023 (Asia Economy via Daum).

Industry reports say Korean brands take about 8 months from concept to launch (Nate News). Global groups find that speed hard to build in-house.

Private equity is buying too. In 2025, KKR reportedly bought ODM supplier Samhwa for ₩733B, and Macquarie PE agreed to buy Hwasung Cosmetics and Nowcos for about ₩300B (PE Insights). K-beauty supply chains were reportedly a main topic at the BDA Partners PE Conference in Hong Kong on 28–29 October.

For buyers: When a supplier's brand or ODM changes owner, check MOQs, pricing and launch timelines again. The 3CE figures show that growth can slow after an acquisition.

4. Dr.Jart+ names its first North America ambassador

According to Cosmetics Business, Dr.Jart+ announced on 30 October that KATSEYE's Yoonchae Jeung is its first North America brand ambassador. Her first campaign is for the Premium BB Cream, which reportedly comes in 8 shades. The regional deal adds to the brand's 2023 global ambassador deal with ENHYPEN (PR Newswire).

The announcement itself is already widely covered. The part that matters more is Canada: Korean cosmetics exports there grew 124.4% to $285M in Jan–Sep.

For buyers: Retailers in both the US and Canada should plan BB cream shade depth and in-store visibility around this campaign.
