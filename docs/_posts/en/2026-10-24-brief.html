The short version

Korean suppliers are lining up for the US. Finding brands isn't the hard part for buyers anymore. The edge is in follow-up terms and compliance paperwork.

Olive Young now invests in some of the brands it distributes, and that changes how its curated assortments get built.

1. LA fair: 400+ meetings, no dollar figure yet [Export data]

KITA and Expoforum ran KOREA EXPO LA 2026, the first North American K-Premium Consumer Goods Fair, at LA's Magic Box from Oct 22 to 24 local time. 85 Korean companies in cosmetics, fashion, food and household goods filled 120 booths. (Metro Seoul)

Around 120 US buyers came, including H Mart, Sally Beauty and Del Sol. They logged more than 400 export meetings over the three days. (Metro Seoul)

KITA trade data shown at the fair put Korea's cosmetics exports to the US up more than 40% YoY for January to September 2026. That is KITA's figure, not MFDS's. (Metro Seoul)

No deal value has been released for LA. For comparison, KITA's 2025 Paris fair reportedly logged 540 meetings with 168 buyers from 29 countries, worth $22.76M. (Youth Daily)

  • Buyer takeaway: Ask for samples, FOB pricing, MOQ and MoCRA facility and product-listing proof within two weeks. A supplier that can't send that pack isn't ready for US shelves.

2. Olive Young puts 8.5bn won into four brands, two of which sit in its Sephora Edit [Deals]

From 2025 through H1 2026, CJ Olive Young invested 8.5bn won in four brand operators:

  • Modogrove (Thorhop): 0.5bn
  • Cosmento Korea (Heavblue): 2.0bn cumulative
  • Olive International (Seongbun Editor, Milktouch, Mommy Care): 3.0bn
  • Bytelab (Lilyeve): 3.0bn

Heavblue and Seongbun Editor are both in the "Olive Young K-Beauty Edit," which launched at Sephora US in August 2026. The Edit is also sold online and in stores at Sephora in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Hong Kong. (BusinessPost)

Olive Young is building its own US store network too. Per a May report, its first US store in Pasadena carries about 400 brands and more than 5,000 SKUs. The target is five US stores by H1 of the following year, served by a 3,600㎡ West Coast logistics center in Bloomington, CA, built in March. (CEO Score Daily)

  • Buyer takeaway: Read an Olive Young pick as a strong signal, not a neutral one. Some Edit brands have Olive Young as a shareholder. Check Korean sell-through data yourself before you mirror the assortment.

3. Re2O US exclusive: at least $60M, regulatory path unclear [Regulation]

On Oct 12, L&C Bio signed an exclusive US supply deal for its Re2O series that runs to the end of 2031. The minimum is about $60M (≈KRW 82bn), based on minimum purchase volumes of Re2O 150mg. Re2O Fine and Re2O V are not covered. (Dailypharm)

Minimum purchases start at about $0.9M in 2026, then step up to $7.2M (2027), $10.8M (2028), $11.7M (2029), $14.4M (2030) and $16.2M (2031). Full supply begins in 2027. FDA clearance status is unclear. (Dailypharm, Business Insider)

The product is already selling at home (Business Insider):

  • H1 2026 Re2O sales: over $17M, with about $11M operating profit, against a ~$326K loss a year earlier.
  • Units sold: 700,000 as of September 2026.
  • L&C Bio quarterly revenue: KRW 17.7bn (Q1 2025) to KRW 38.8bn (Q2 2026).
  • Market: Yuanta Securities projects Korea's skin-derived ECM injectable market at $75M in 2026, with Re2O holding an estimated 60-70%.

Supply is a structural risk. MFDS figures put about 90% of the human tissue used in Korea as imported. L&C Bio bought more than $25M of US donor skin in 2023-2025 and another $3.8M in H1 2026. (Business Insider) Per an Oct 13 filing, it priced a 144.67bn-won rights offering at 39,100 won per share, with 105bn won going to a new Dongtan plant. (Dailypharm)

  • Buyer takeaway: Medspa distributors looking at Korean boosters should get the FDA classification and pathway in writing before committing to minimum volumes.

4. AI buyer matching for K-beauty SMEs, starting in Vietnam [Channels]

Trade-AI startup Waion was selected for KIBO's "KIBO Star Valley" program on Oct 15. It plans to expand its AI buyer matching (LeadNow) and webinars (WebiNow) for K-beauty and K-food SMEs entering Southeast Asia, starting with Vietnam. (Special Times)

  • Buyer takeaway: Southeast Asian distributors should expect more inbound pitches from Korean SMEs. Set your intake criteria (MOQ, margin, registration status) now.
