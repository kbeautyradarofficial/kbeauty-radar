Bottom line: PDRN isn't a K-beauty exclusive anymore. The Ordinary sells a vegan 4% PDRN eye cream for $9.50 on its US site (checked in October). 'Contains PDRN' no longer justifies a price premium. Korean brands are already splitting the market three ways: ingredient source (plant-based), costly new actives (blue peptide), and low-price or advice-led channels (Daiso, pharmacies).

1. The Ordinary's 4% PDRN resets the price anchor at $9.50

The Ordinary's 15ml eye cream pairs 4% vegan Lacto-PDRN, made by fermenting Lactobacillus rhamnosus, with vitamin B9 (folic acid) and a film-former. It went on sale in Korea on Aug 28 via its Naver brand store and Yeonnam store (CosinKorea). The US price is $9.50 (The Ordinary US site, as of October).

It comes with data. Per Beauty Economy, a 32-person test showed 72% hydration over two hours after use. Twice-daily use for eight weeks showed 18% improvement in under-eye aging signs and 17% in eyelid texture.

For buyers: any K-beauty PDRN product priced well above $9.50 now needs a clear reason, whether that's concentration, source or test size and duration. Ask for it before you list.

2. Mugwort instead of salmon: the source is the story

Aekyung Industrial presented mugwort-derived plant PDRN research as a poster at IFSCC 2026 in Perth, Australia (Sept 28-Oct 1), per Money Today and The K Beauty Science. The mugwort material has a Korean patent and a PCT filing. Five plant PDRN materials, including Brussels sprout and birch, hold Korean patents, and some already appear in the makeup brand AGE20'S.

Two signals. Both The Ordinary (fermentation) and Aekyung (plants) lead with 'not salmon'. And PDRN is now moving from skincare into makeup SKUs.

3. What's after PDRN: blue peptide, azelaic acid, TXA, Volufiline

According to Sisa Journal-e (Sept 22), TOUN28 built its 10th-anniversary line around a roughly 400-dalton GHK-Cu copper peptide. TOUN28 says the raw material costs about ₩18M per kg. A skin booster, serum and cream step up from 0.3% to 0.5% to 1.0%. The brand reported an average 45.7% gain across five firmness measures for the cream in its own testing. The same report says Anua is pushing azelaic acid, and Anua, Celimax and Goodal have launched TXA products.

There's a US signal too. Asia Economy (Sept 30) reported US searches for the volumizing active Volufiline rose 1,200% in a year. Medicube sells a Vita A retinal volume booster serum on its US site.

4. Pharmacies and Daiso: how fast new actives trickle down

Alico Pharm launched a gromwell-derived PDRN repair cream for pharmacies (PharmNews, reported Oct 12). Hanmi Science released a pharmacy retinoid cream in early October (InThe News). Tonymoly took new NAD+ products to Daiso in September (Joseilbo, Sept 15).

Our read: the cycle from dermatology clinic to premium home care to pharmacy and Daiso keeps getting shorter. So does any premium based only on the ingredient name.

What buyers should do next quarter

  • Ask PDRN vendors for the source (salmon, plant or fermentation), concentration, and test sample size and duration
  • Benchmark PDRN retail prices against The Ordinary's $9.50 and make vendors justify the gap
  • For North America and Europe, give vegan PDRN SKUs priority in your range
  • Favor tiered-strength lines (e.g. 0.3/0.5/1.0%) that build basket size and trade-up
  • Check for channel price conflicts: the same active may already sell at pharmacy or Daiso prices in Korea
