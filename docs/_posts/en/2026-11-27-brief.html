1. APR buys 87.3% of Dr. Melaxin for about ₩390bn

APR disclosed on Nov 26 that it will acquire 87.3% of Brand501, the company behind Dr. Melaxin, for about ₩390bn in total: roughly ₩320bn for 74.35% of existing shares and ₩70bn for newly issued shares (12.95%). (Asia Economy)

Charm.io data puts Dr. Melaxin at #2 by sales among beauty brands on US TikTok Shop in H1 2026. (Asia Economy) Korea Report says the deal is expected to close on Dec 31, 2026, when Brand501 becomes a consolidated APR subsidiary. (Korea Report) According to Money Today, Daol Investment & Securities analyst Park Jong-hyun says the price was agreed as Dr. Melaxin's US TikTok Shop growth was slowing, which put it below the valuation the market had been discussing. (Money Today)

  • For distributors: Dr. Melaxin is moving under a group with its own channel plans. Expect a push beyond TikTok Shop into Amazon and offline, and expect your terms to be reviewed.
  • For anyone pricing K-indie acquisitions: in this deal, a top TikTok Shop rank did not protect the price once growth slowed.

2. Missha Japan +28%, and one cushion is 63% of it

Able C&C (Missha) reported Q3 2026 revenue of ₩57.9bn, up 2.8% YoY. Operating profit was ₩6.5bn, an 11.2% margin. (Herald Business)

According to the same report, Japan sales rose 28% YoY (33% in yen). The M Cushion line makes up 63% of Japan sales and grew about 30% in local currency. Overseas sales rose 24.2% in Q3 and account for 73% of 2026 sales to date. Excluding the Korean direct stores it fully exited in August, growth was 20%. (Herald Business)

  • This is a Japan cushion story, not a group turnaround. One hero SKU is carrying an entire market.
  • For buyers: when you take on a K-brand, list its proven hero product first and add line extensions once it sells through. (Missha on Qoo10)

3. Olive Young moves 10 of 30 rookie brands to its Global Mall

Olive Young's third K-Super Rookie with Young cohort, run with Korea's Ministry of SMEs and Startups, reportedly grew to 30 companies from 20 in 2024. From Oct 1, entrants spent 6 weeks on "Olive Young New" shelves in 23 stores. (Herald)

The top 10, picked on customer response, sales and growth potential, sell on Olive Young's domestic online mall and Global Mall from Nov 16 to year-end, with online promotions, live commerce and post-program consulting. (CJ Newsroom)

  • For sourcing teams: these 10 were filtered on real in-store sell-through in Korea. Treat it as a ready-made scouting shortlist.

4. Online K-beauty reaches $15bn, and Europe's share is now 10%

Euromonitor sizes 2025 online K-beauty sales across 17 major countries at US$15bn, up about 22% from US$12.5bn in 2024. In 2025 the US accounts for 54%. Europe's share rose from 3% to 10%, and China's fell from 66% to 21%. On US TikTok Shop, K-beauty climbed from 27th in 2023 to 7th in 2025, and its beauty and personal care sales grew from under US$100m to over US$900m. (Euromonitor)

  • A "6.1% global share by 2027" figure is circulating, but we could not trace it to a source. For a European K-beauty case, use Euromonitor's Europe share (3% to 10%) instead.
  • For EU retailers: ask Korean suppliers early whether their SKUs are CPNP-ready. That lead time is where European launches tend to slip.
