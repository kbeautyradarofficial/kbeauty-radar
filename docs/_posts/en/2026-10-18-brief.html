The US remains K-beauty's growth engine, but scale brings policy exposure. Here is what sourcing teams, distributors and retailers should watch this week.

1. K-beauty exports to the US up 14%

An analysis of K-beauty's three largest export markets shows shipments to the US grew 14%, with the cosmetics trade balance widening 17.0% (Beauty Economy). Korean industry coverage also links rising exports to expectations of stronger Q4 results (CosinKorea).

Why it matters: Demand from US consumers continues to pull Korean supply, which can tighten allocation on fast-moving SKUs.

Takeaway: Lock in holiday and Q1 purchase orders early with Korean suppliers for top-selling lines.

2. Korea's US trade surplus approaches $100B

Korea's trade surplus with the US is nearing $100 billion, prompting concern that the Trump administration could step up tariff pressure (Aju Business Daily).

Why it matters: Landed costs on Korean beauty imports could shift if trade measures change.

Takeaway: Agree on tariff pass-through and price-review clauses with Korean vendors before signing 2027 contracts.

3. Chinese cosmetics move upmarket, starting with color

Chinese beauty brands are shedding their budget positioning and pushing into K-beauty's territory, beginning with color cosmetics (Yonhap Infomax).

Why it matters: Buyers will have more Asian makeup options competing for the same shelf and marketplace slots.

Takeaway: Benchmark Korean and Chinese color lines side by side on shade range, formula and margin before resetting assortments.

4. Centellian24 posts record on US Amazon

Centellian24 achieved its best performance to date on Amazon US, while sister brand news included a large outdoor campaign for AGE20'S in Seoul (Singlelist). More broadly, K-beauty is evolving toward derma, with pharmaceutical and medical-device companies joining the skin-science race (Newspim).

Why it matters: Ingredient-led, pharma-backed K-derma brands are proving traction with US online shoppers.

Takeaway: Add a dedicated K-derma segment to your sourcing shortlist and request clinical or efficacy documentation from suppliers.

5. Beauty expo logs 1,200 export meetings in three days

A Korean beauty expo bringing together domestic and international cosmetics companies recorded 1,200 export consultations over three days (JoongAng Ilbo).

Why it matters: Trade shows remain a high-density channel for discovering emerging Korean brands before they scale.

Takeaway: Plan a Korea sourcing trip around the next expo cycle with pre-booked supplier meetings.
