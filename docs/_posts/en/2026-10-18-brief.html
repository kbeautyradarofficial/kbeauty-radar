1. 30-minute beauty delivery arrives on Korea's food apps

Off Beauty, a discount cosmetics retailer, now delivers in about 30 minutes through Baemin and Coupang Eats. As of Oct 14, 2026, its Baemin listing covers around 4,000 products shipped from 30 stores in Seoul, Busan and other cities. It launched on Coupang Eats first, in June 2026. The chain has about 50 stores and is aiming for 100. The Korea Herald

The benchmark is Olive Young's Oneul Deurim, which launched in 2018 and usually delivers within three hours. Its fastest tier has averaged 55 minutes this year. Orders ship from about 1,360 stores and urban fulfillment centers. The service handled 19.35 million deliveries in 2025. In H1 2026, Olive Young booked ₩2.94 trillion in beauty sales. That is a record 21.5% share of Korea's ₩13.72 trillion beauty retail market. Government data shows online sales made up 41.4% of Korean cosmetics sales in 2025. The Korea Herald

For buyers: Discounted K-beauty stock now moves through instant-delivery apps inside Korea. If you benchmark against Korean domestic retail, check discount and quick-commerce prices too, not just Olive Young. Ask your brand partners which SKUs they keep out of discount channels.

2. Medical tourists now pick dermatology over plastic surgery: 67.5% vs 21.3%

BC Card data shows dermatology took 67.5% of foreign visitors' medical card spending in H1 2026. Plastic surgery took 21.3%. Two years earlier, in H1 2024, surgery led with 46.2% and dermatology had 32.6%. Year on year, dermatology spending rose 109.8%, plastic surgery 35.5% and total medical spending 98%. The Star / Korea Herald (ANN)

Spending is also shifting within Seoul. Gangnam's share fell 10.4 points to 39.8%, and Seocho's rose 6.7 points to 27.6% (H1 2026). The Culture Ministry estimates foreign visitors spent ₩10.04 trillion in Korea in H1 2026, up 50.8%. The Star / Korea Herald (ANN)

For buyers: Expect "clinic-grade" and post-procedure homecare claims to keep drawing traffic. Shortlist the barrier-repair and soothing lines that brands already sell near Korean dermatology clinics.

3. Pharmacy spending +1,176.9%, but only a 2% share

Foreign-card spending at Korean pharmacies rose 1,176.9% YoY in H1 2026. Pharmacies still made up only 2% of medical card spending, up from 0.3% (BC Card definition). The Star / Korea Herald (ANN)

Counting transactions gives a very different number. One report says pharmacies' share of foreign medical cases rose from 38.24% in 2021 to 59.11% in 2025. That puts them ahead of dermatology at 22.07%. Total cases came to about 4.16 million in 2025. Daum

For buyers: A high share of transactions with a low share of spending means a frequent, low-ticket channel. Pharmacy cosmeceuticals that sell well to tourists in Korea are more likely to work abroad as affordable, high-turnover lines than as premium hero products.

4. ODM capacity: Kolmar up about 1.8x, but sunscreen slots stay tight

Reports say Korea Kolmar's standalone capacity grew from 430 million units in 2024 to 530 million in 2025 and 770 million in 2026. As of April 2026, the same report said its Sejong plant supplies about 4,500 clients and can make 890 million skincare units a year. All of Kolmar's sunscreen is made at Sejong. The plant's expansion is due to finish in H2 2027. Herald Economy

For buyers: Sun care slots will likely stay tight until the 2027 capacity comes online. Place spring/summer 2027 sun care orders early, and confirm production windows and lead times with your brand partners this quarter.
