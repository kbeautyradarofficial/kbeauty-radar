1. Silicon2: demand held up, FX took the profit

Silicon2 reported preliminary Q3 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 424.4bn, up 41.7% year on year. That is its seventh record quarter in a row (Daehan Economy). Operating profit rose 10.4% to KRW 69.6bn (CBC News).

Net profit dropped 84.9%, from KRW 58.4bn to KRW 8.8bn. Non-operating results swung from a KRW 7.7bn gain to a KRW 47.5bn loss, and most of that loss came from FX as the won strengthened (Joseilbo). Operating margin fell from 21.1% to 16.4%. The company says some of the unrealized translation loss may reverse later (Lead Economy).

  • Revenue growth shows demand for K-beauty through this distributor is still strong. The squeeze is on margins.
  • For buyers: if the won stays strong, Korean suppliers who invoice in USD may ask to reprice or change payment terms. Plan for that conversation before 2027 pricing is locked.

2. Kolmar Holdings: revenue down 18.7%, margin up to 14.5%

Kolmar Holdings posted preliminary Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 134.5bn, down 18.7%. Operating profit rose 19.5% to KRW 19.5bn (News1). Operating margin rose from 9.9% to 14.5%, and net profit was up 68.8% at KRW 9bn. For January to September, revenue was KRW 428.8bn (-13.1%) and operating profit KRW 65.3bn (+82.1%). Revenue fell because of restructuring, including moving HNG's cosmetics division and Kolmar SK to other group companies (Digital Daily).

Quarter on quarter, revenue fell 5.5% and operating profit fell 23.0% versus Q2 (Digital Daily).

  • The margin gain comes from restructuring, so it says little about how busy K-beauty ODM lines are.
  • If you source private label through Kolmar group entities, check which legal entity holds your contract now.

3. A proposed US K-beauty ETF counts aesthetics as K-beauty

On July 16, 2026 (US time), Guinness Atkinson filed a preliminary prospectus with the SEC for the Guinness Atkinson K-Beauty ETF (ticker KBTY). The fund would invest at least 80% of net assets in beauty companies headquartered or listed in Korea, and it aimed to list on NYSE Arca in the second half of 2026 (Cosinkorea).

Besides cosmetics brands, ODMs and distributors, eligible holdings include makers of botulinum toxin, fillers, skin boosters and aesthetic devices. As of the July filing, holdings, weights and fees had not been disclosed (Cosinkorea). We have not confirmed that the fund has listed.

  • US capital markets are defining K-beauty more broadly than skincare. Retailers planning K-beauty sections can use the same scope and include devices and derm-adjacent products.
  • This is not investment advice.

4. Coupang Taiwan: Korean SMEs reportedly up 81%

Media reports say about 30 SMEs in Coupang's joint expansion program grew their Taiwan transaction value 81% year on year. Sources gave different reference periods (one cited April to December 2025), and we could not check the original article. The product mix is reportedly widening from K-beauty into household goods and mobile accessories (Bizworld). A March 2026 report said Coupang opened a fourth fulfillment center in Taoyuan, which extends Rocket Delivery to about 70% of Taiwan (Digital Daily).

  • Taiwan distributors and retailers now compete with a platform that delivers Korean SKUs straight to consumers, and delivers them fast.
  • On hero SKUs you share with Coupang, compare your landed price and delivery time with theirs.
