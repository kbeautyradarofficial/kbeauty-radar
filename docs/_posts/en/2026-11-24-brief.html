1. Korean SME cosmetics exports +28.6% in Q3, with growth shifting beyond the US

Korean SME cosmetics exports were US$2.79bn in Q3 2026 (Jul–Sep), up 28.6% year on year. The Jan–Sep total was US$7.65bn (+26.4%). These are Ministry of SMEs and Startups figures for small and mid-sized exporters only, not total Korean cosmetics exports. (Korea Policy Briefing / MSS)

By region, Q3 SME cosmetics exports grew 141.3% to Latin America, 58.6% to Europe and 33.1% to North America. US shipments alone were US$560m, up 14.5%, the slowest of the group. (Korea Policy Briefing)

The US is still the biggest market. Across all product categories, it was the top SME export market in Q3 at US$5.73bn (+19.6%), with outlets crediting K-beauty's wider presence in major US retail chains. (Korea Policy Briefing) In cosmetics, though, the fastest growth is now in smaller markets.

For context, reports put H1 2026 SME cosmetics exports at US$5.069bn (+30.7%), or 7.9% of all SME exports. (Money Today)

  • For LatAm and EU buyers: Korean SME brands are actively growing in your regions. If you want exclusivity or priority allocation, open those talks before brands finalise 2027 plans.

2. Online cosmetics exports nearly doubled: UK +729.6%, Netherlands +515.3%

SME online cosmetics exports reached US$360m in Q3 2026, up 97.5%, and made up 75.3% of all SME online exports. Online exports to the UK rose 729.6% and to the Netherlands 515.3%. (Korea Policy Briefing)

The Netherlands is a common EU fulfilment hub, so part of this is likely cross-border volume serving the wider EU, not only Dutch demand. It suggests a route where brands prove European sell-through online before signing traditional distributors.

  • For EU retailers: ask Korean brands for their marketplace sell-through data before a first order. It tells you more about reorder potential than a pitch deck.

3. China cuts special-cosmetics change reviews to 60 and 45 working days

China's NIDC (under NMPA) announced on 9 Nov 2026 a three-tier system for changes to special-cosmetics registrations, applying to change applications accepted from 20 Nov 2026. (CIRS Group)

Technical review limits drop from 90 to 60 working days for major changes and from 90 to 45 for moderate ones. Minor changes are self-maintained by the company in the online system, at most once every 30 days. (CIRS Group)

  • For China importers: whitening and sunscreen SKUs with pending reformulations or label updates could reach shelf sooner. Ask your Korean suppliers which tier each change falls under.

4. ADbiotech takes 51% of Kazakhstan K-beauty platform Bestee's Korean entity

KOSDAQ-listed ADbiotech (애드바이오텍) said on 19 Nov 2026 it acquired 51% of the Korean distribution entity of Bestee, a Kazakhstan K-beauty platform, following an October 2026 cooperation agreement. (Asia Economy)

Reports say Almaty-based Bestee carries 700+ products. ADbiotech sources Korean brands; Bestee handles local marketing, distribution and sales. (Dailypharm)

  • For Central Asian distributors: a platform now has a Korea-based sourcing arm, which may change how brands allocate stock in the region. In any brand deal, confirm who covers EAEU certification costs.

5. Musinsa Beauty Seongsu opens with about 700 brands

According to reports, Musinsa Beauty's second beauty-only store opened in Seongsu on 11 Nov 2026 with about 700 brands and a pharmacist-staffed beauty pharmacy. It is Musinsa's 100th offline store, after Musinsa Beauty Hongdae opened in September. (Byline Network)

Musinsa Beauty also works as a pre-launch channel. Reports say ABLE C&C launched three MISSHA PDRN·NAD+ skincare products there first in April 2026. (Cosinkorea)

  • For sourcing teams: track Musinsa Beauty pre-launches as an early read on which Korean SKUs Gen Z picks up before they reach Amazon or Ulta.
