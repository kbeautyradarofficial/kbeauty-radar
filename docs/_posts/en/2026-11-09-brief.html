1. A Korean delegation is coming to Brazil, and ANVISA will be in the room

Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) will take a joint public-private K-beauty delegation to Brazil on Feb 24-26, 2027. Day 1 is a pop-up store with buyer meetings and a forum. Day 2 is a heads-of-agency meeting with ANVISA. Day 3 is a regulatory roundtable where companies can put questions to ANVISA officials directly. (Financial News)

Korean brands apply through the Korea Cosmetic Association. The deadline is the 23rd; the article gives no month, and we read it as Nov 23. Wide Daily reports that about 20 companies will be selected, with results due Nov 24, and that KOTRA São Paulo and the KITA São Paulo office are co-organizing. The same outlet puts Korean cosmetics exports to Brazil at $9M in 2022 and $54M in 2025, about 6x. It does not say which agency compiled the figures. MFDS calls Brazil the world's 3rd-largest cosmetics market.

For Brazilian importers and distributors: the brands arriving in February will have just heard ANVISA's guidance in person. That makes it a good time to settle importer-of-record terms and filing timelines. Request Day 1 meetings early.

2. Colombia: +42% in 2025, and an NSO lasts 7 years

Colombia's imports of Korean cosmetics under HS 3304 rose from $7.48M in 2024 to $10.62M in 2025, up 42%. At KOTRA's Medellín event on Aug 21, 2026, 17 Korean companies took part, including APR, Musinsa and Cosmax. The event offered INVIMA certification consulting. A manual review can take up to 3 months, and an NSO certificate stays valid for 7 years. (CNC NEWS)

For Andean buyers: the base is still small. Allow up to 3 months for INVIMA review before the first PO, and put hero SKUs into review first.

3. Olive Young US: 2 stores open, 3 more planned in California

Olive Young's first stores outside Korea are in Pasadena and at Westfield Century City. Citing Bloomberg, Bisnow reported on June 24, 2026 that three more California stores are coming in the next few months. The company runs a 39,000 sq ft hub in Bloomington, CA and plans to sign for an East Coast hub later in 2026. Its goal is for overseas customers to make up 90% of sales within ten years, up from about 30% now.

Fitt Insider reports that the Pasadena flagship opened in May 2026 with about 5,000 products from 400 brands, and that New York follows five more California stores. That count differs from Bloomberg's three, so treat the rollout as a plan, not a schedule.

For US retailers: a Korea-native specialty chain with deep K-beauty assortment is coming to your market. Lock in exclusives or early launch windows with your top K-beauty suppliers before an East Coast hub makes national fulfillment easier for Olive Young.

4. Tourists are propping up Korean retail

Korea had 10.71M foreign visitors in H1 2026, up more than 21%, and their spending topped ₩12T, according to Korea JoongAng Daily. Domestic retail has been uneven: sales fell 1.8% month on month in August 2026 and rose 3.6% in September, per Trading Economics. Olive Young's ~30% overseas share includes tourists shopping in Korea. Citing Yonhap, Fitt Insider puts the 2025 offline figure at 28%.

For buyers: strong sell-through in Seoul does not prove demand in your market. When you vet a brand, ask for sales data from channels outside Korea.
