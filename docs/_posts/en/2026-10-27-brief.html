1. USD 11.1bn in nine months, with the US at 21.1%

Provisional MFDS data puts Korea's cosmetics exports for January–September 2026 at USD 11.1bn, up 31.1% YoY. No earlier Jan–Sep period was higher. The same months totaled USD 6.0bn in 2022, 6.2bn in 2023, 7.4bn in 2024 and 8.5bn in 2025. (CosinKorea)

  • By destination (Jan–Sep): US USD 2.35bn (21.1%), China USD 1.58bn (14.2%), Japan USD 0.89bn (8.0%). The UK, Netherlands and Poland were named as growth markets.
  • Skincare led at USD 8.72bn.
  • Q3 on its own came to USD 4.17bn, 7.5% above Q2 (USD 3.88bn).

Reported provisional exports for full-year 2025 were about USD 11.4bn (+12.3%). The first nine months of 2026 nearly match that. (CosinKorea)

For buyers: Supply is growing, but Q3 was up only 7.5% on Q2. Plan Q4 and Q1 orders from your own sell-through, not from the headline growth rate.

2. Amazon: only 1 in 5 beauty shoppers buys K-beauty

At Amazon Beauty in Seoul 2026 (Oct 27), Amazon announced four projects. It will develop 7 leading K-beauty brands over three years. In 2027 it plans to bring in 50 new hair and wellness sellers with 200 new products and help about 100 K-beauty brands enter Europe. (News1)

  • According to Amazon, Korean sellers' beauty sales roughly doubled YoY in Jan–Sep, and K-beauty is growing more than 3x as fast as beauty overall. (BizWatch)
  • Amazon says one in five of its beauty customers buys K-beauty and treats the other ~80% as untapped demand. (Kuki News)
  • Reports describe two of the programs. "Project Million" fully supports 50 new sellers that aim for USD 1M in first-year sales, covering planning, listing, logistics and marketing. "Project Autobahn" handles tax registration, certification, compliance and multilingual listings in 5 EU countries. (Herald Korea US)

For buyers: Expect more Korean brands to reach Europe, and to show up in hair and wellness, already selling on Amazon. If you distribute in the EU, ask brands about their Amazon plans early. Exclusivity and pricing terms get harder to win once a brand sells direct.

3. Europe: compliance decides who gets in

Amazon's goal of about 100 brands entering Europe in 2027 lines up with the export data, which named the UK, Netherlands and Poland as growth markets. (News1, CosinKorea)

For buyers: Before you commit to a Korean line for the EU, check CPNP notification, the Responsible Person and local-language labels for every SKU. Distributors that can act as RP have leverage now.

4. Olive Young's edit is in 580+ US Sephora stores

Reports say CJ Olive Young's "K-Beauty Edit" launched on Aug 20 in more than 580 US Sephora stores and on Sephora.com. Olive Young's own figure is 500+. The edit has about 150 SKUs, mostly skincare, from 19 brands including FULLY. (Korea JoongAng Daily) According to Olive Young, Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Hong Kong come next by end-2026, followed by Canada, Australia, the UK and the Middle East in 2027. (Olive Young release via Webull)

For buyers: If you sell in the 2027 markets, Sephora will likely have a curated Korean assortment. Look for brands outside the edit, or formats that don't overlap with it.

5. US stores split by price tier

Dollar-price beauty chain Miss A plans to go from about 100 US stores to 550 and reach USD 1bn in annual sales by 2030 (company targets). (Cap Times) In Coresight's week 42 tracker, K-beauty retailer Moida opened 11 stores, more than any other US retailer that week. (Coresight Research)

For buyers: Specialty stores (Sephora), K-beauty specialists (Moida) and dollar stores (Miss A) each need their own pack sizes and margins. Ask suppliers for channel-specific SKUs so the same item doesn't carry three prices in one mall.
