1. Korea-China FTA phase 2 is done, with zero cosmetics-specific measures

Korea and China announced on Nov 18, at their APEC summit in Shenzhen, that they had substantially settled the FTA follow-up talks on services and investment. Talks started in March 2018, so this took 8 years and 8 months. The deal opens up services such as content, tourism and distribution. It has no measure aimed at cosmetics. (Financial News)

Stocks moved anyway. As of 10:50 KST on Nov 19, Hankook Cosmetics was up 29.98% to 6,460 won and Beautyskin was up 29.86% to 2,005 won. Both hit their daily limit. (Financial News) At 10:21 KST, Hankook Cosmetics Manufacturing was up 19.13% and Tonymoly 12.34%, while larger names lagged: d'Alba Global +3.95% and LG H&H +2.25%. (Hankyung) Reports say analysts expect little direct earnings impact, because Korean makers now rely much less on China. (coverage roundup) One report says cosmetics exports to China rose for five straight months from June to October, but no values have been confirmed. (Global Economic)

For buyers, traffic matters more than tariffs here. Both governments want people-to-people exchanges back to about 13 million in 2027, the 2016 level. (Financial News) If tourist flows recover, Korean duty-free stores and Seoul street shops will see demand first. Wider access for distribution services could also make it easier for Korean brands to run their own China entities instead of leaning on daigou and cross-border resellers.

  • Buyer takeaway: if you source K-beauty for China or travel retail, expect brands to shift hero-SKU allocation toward duty-free and direct channels in 2027. Lock in your allocation terms early.

2. Nooni: #1 in three Amazon categories, then 1,778 Target doors

Mimibox's Nooni Apple Lip Oil has reportedly sold more than 5.1 million units worldwide. (VentureSquare) The brand is reported to be in 1,778 Target stores plus Target.com and 89 Nordstrom stores. In July and August it reportedly ranked #1 in three Amazon US categories: Lip Gloss, Lip Plumping Treatments and Lip Care. (VentureSquare)

The pattern: one hero SKU builds marketplace rank, and that rank gives brick-and-mortar buyers a reason to say yes.

  • Buyer takeaway: Amazon category ranks are a useful early filter for K-beauty SKUs that are ready for retail. Before you commit to more doors, ask about replenishment lead times and MOQ.

3. A regional Korean maker reaches Türkiye through a Chinese MLM network

According to News1, Namwon-based Coviks signed a 0.8bn-won initial supply deal in July with China's TIENS Group, which operates in 224 countries. The deal covers serums, toners, ampoules and eye creams. This month's Türkiye shipment of 8 SKUs reportedly goes through TIENS's European branch. Coviks expects more than 20bn won in sales through TIENS channels next year. (News1)

  • Buyer takeaway: in Türkiye, the CIS and Central Asia, K-beauty supply often comes through multi-country distributors rather than platforms. Check territory exclusivity before you sign with a maker that already has an MLM partner.

4. AddBiotech takes 51% of Kazakh K-beauty platform Vesti

AddBiotech says it is acquiring 51% of the Korean entity of Vesti, a K-beauty platform in Kazakhstan. The deal terms and a regulatory filing could not be confirmed, and the company's planned share issue has reportedly been delayed. (Money Today / Green Economy)

  • Buyer takeaway: Korean capital is starting to buy into Central Asian channels. Retailers in the region should expect more competition from platforms with Korean owners.
