1. Korean SME cosmetics exports: $2.79B in Q3, +28.6%

Ministry of SMEs data shows Korean SME cosmetics exports reached $2.79B in Q3 2026 (Jul–Sep), up 28.6% year on year. January–September totaled $7.65B, up 26.4%. (Hankyung)

Look at the base. Q3 2025 was already $2.21B, up 28.0%. (Asiae) That makes this a second straight year of roughly 28% growth on a bigger base. Across all categories, SME exports were $33.4B in Q3 (+8.8%), the highest for any third quarter. Cosmetics grew more than three times faster. (Newsfreezone)

  • For buyers: This figure covers small and mid-sized companies only, not all Korean cosmetics. That makes it a useful signal for the indie brands you are most likely sourcing.

2. Online exports nearly doubled; Latin America +141%

SME cosmetics sold online jumped 97.5% to $360M in Q3 2026. Basic skincare rose 35.1% to $1.26B. (Asiae) By region, reported Q3 growth was +33.1% for North America, +58.6% for Europe (Asiae reports 56.1%) and +141.3% for Latin America. (Hankyung)

  • For buyers: Cross-border sales now test demand before distributors commit. If you cover LatAm, find the SKUs already reordering through cross-border channels and pursue local distribution for those first.

3. Proya enters 400 Ulta stores in November

China's Proya will launch skincare in 400 Ulta Beauty stores and on Ulta's online store in November 2026. Florasis has been sold in some Ulta UAE stores since September. A.S. Watson, with about 17,000 stores in 31 markets, launched BrandLab to incubate C- and J-beauty brands alongside K-beauty. At Scorsh's New York flagship, Chinese products now make up 15% of beauty items, up from 5% two years ago. (Seoul Economic Daily)

China's cosmetics exports reached $7.82B in 2025, up from $4.84B in 2021. Ulta CEO Kecia Steelman still reported double-digit K-beauty sales growth in Q2. (Seoul Economic Daily)

  • For retailers: You have more Asian skincare options at the table now. Bring that to 2027 planogram reviews and ask K-brands for exclusive SKUs or holiday sets in exchange for continued space.

4. L'Oréal: "The K-beauty boom is real" — at 3–4% share

On L'Oréal's Q3 2026 call, CEO Nicolas Hieronimus cited 30% average growth for K-beauty brands and estimated K-beauty's share of global skincare at 3–4%. (Hankyung) A L'Oréal Korea release says Cosmax and L'Oréal signed an MOU in Paris on Sep 8, 2026, covering joint R&D, new actives and K-beauty-inspired formats. Cosmax has supplied L'Oréal since 2004. (L'Oréal Korea)

Reported FnGuide consensus for Q3 2026 puts Cosmax operating profit at ₩71.2B (+66.7%) and Kolmar Korea at ₩92.5B (+58.6%). Results are not out yet. (Global Economic)

  • For buyers: Global majors are co-developing K-style formats with Korean manufacturers (ODMs), so the K-beauty look is no longer exclusive to Korean labels. Judge suppliers on brand equity and sell-through, not formula claims alone, and expect longer ODM lead times.
