The short version: capital is buying factories, channels and distressed brands

Korean cosmetics exports reached $11.1B in January–September 2026 (MFDS preliminary), up 31.1% year on year. That is the highest total on record for the first three quarters (ETNews). The US was the top destination over the same period at $2.35B, or 21.1% of the total (FoodToday).

Capital has followed. Over the past year, most of the largest deals went to ODMs, to stakes bought by groups from outside beauty, to brands in receivership and to roll-up platforms. Few went to single indie brands.

1. PE bought the factories: about ₩300B

In September 2026, Macquarie Asset Management and Affirma Capital bought 70% of color-cosmetics ODM Hwasung Cosmetic and 100% of skincare ODM Nowcos for about ₩300B combined. Their 2025 revenues were about ₩110B and ₩69.5B (Newspim).

According to reports, skincare exports rose 45.1% to $8.72B in January–September 2026, while color cosmetics fell 3.6% to $1.11B (Cosmorning). The buyers took a color ODM anyway, packaged with a skincare one. The bet is that a brand's volume runs through a factory no matter which brand is winning. Meritz is also reportedly pursuing packaging maker Chaewoon Package, though no terms have been confirmed (via THE VC).

For buyers, a change of ODM owner can mean new terms on MOQs, unit pricing and production slots for the brands you carry.

2. Three ways groups from outside beauty are getting in

  • Majority stake: In October 2025, a consortium of Taekwang Industrial, T2 PE and Yuanta Investment bought 63.13% of Aekyung Industrial for about ₩470B (₩28,190 per share). Taekwang has set aside about ₩300B for beauty: about ₩220B for Aekyung, about ₩80B for Dongsung Pharmaceutical (January 2026) and ₩6B for a new entity (Newspim).
  • Convertible bonds: In August 2026, holding company Hyosung bought ₩30B of convertible bonds in the pre-IPO round of K-beauty commerce company 2020, at a valuation of about ₩200B. The company is aiming for an IPO in 2027 (Chosun Biz).
  • Receivership deal: On September 22, 2026, Dongwon Group, through Dongwon Home Food, was named the final preferred bidder for Charmzone, which is being sold under Seoul Bankruptcy Court supervision (CEO Score Daily).

3. Charmzone: paying for the brand name

Charmzone's revenue fell from ₩128.8B in 2022 to ₩18.1B in 2025. It posted operating losses for five straight years (2020–2025), and its equity stood at -₩35.2B at the end of 2025. The price has only been described as "several tens of billions of won" and has not been officially disclosed (CEO Score Daily). Dongwon is paying for brand recognition and channel history, not earnings.

4. Roll-ups and IPOs: intangibles are 59.3% of assets

Goodai Global reported 2025 consolidated revenue of ₩1.47T and operating profit of ₩273.4B. Most of the revenue growth came from consolidating companies it acquired. Intangible assets were ₩1.69T at the end of 2025, or 59.3% of total assets (Daum).

Goodai and Company K Partners bought Round Lab for about ₩600B. The sellers, Calypse Capital and Meritz, had paid about ₩240B in 2023 and recovered about ₩750B from the sale plus dividends (The Fact). According to reports, Goodai's pre-IPO post-money valuation was ₩4.4T, the market now talks about roughly ₩10T, and a KOSPI listing is targeted for early 2027 (IB Tomato).

Wiseplanet (Dr.PL) filed on July 30, 2026 for a KOSDAQ listing with a price band of ₩10,000–12,000, raising ₩16–19.2B (Newsis). Musinsa reportedly filed for KOSPI pre-listing review on September 7, 2026, with valuations of ₩8–10T being discussed (Dealsite).

What to watch next quarter

  • When Charmzone's court process closes, and the official price
  • Whether the ODMs' new owners change pricing or MOQs
  • How goodwill and valuations are handled as the 2027 IPO candidates prepare to list
