1. FDA refusals of Korean cosmetics: 171 by August, past FY2025's total

Korean cosmetics logged 171 FDA import refusals in FY2026 through August 6. That is already more than the 158 for all of FY2025 (Oct 2024–Sep 2025), with nearly two months of the fiscal year still to go. (CNC News, citing KITA U.S. office data) NewsDream used 178 in its headline. That may be a later cumulative count, but it is unconfirmed.

  • In FY2025, Korea accounted for 16.6% of all 951 refusals, second only to India (182).
  • 69% of Korea's FY2025 refusals (109 cases) were about labeling. Most involved claims like sun protection or acne improvement, which turn a cosmetic into an OTC drug.
  • The single company with the most refusals so far in FY2026 is Seoul Cosmetics, with 33.

For importers, this is a supplier-qualification issue. Ask Korean vendors for the final English label before you issue the PO. Flag any SPF, acne or blemish claim unless the product is registered as OTC. A held shipment costs you weeks of shelf time, on top of the paperwork.

2. REJURAN enters Costco's first U.S. Beauty Table program

PharmaResearch's REJURAN COSMETICS is part of Costco's first-ever U.S. Beauty Table program. It is in 25 warehouses today, with plans to expand to more than 150, according to the October 23 announcement. (PR Newswire, company release)

  • The Costco-exclusive SKU is a c-PDRN Turnover Ampoule set: a 50 mL full size plus an extra 10 mL. The release does not give a price.
  • Reports say REJURAN began selling in about 380 U.S. Sephora stores on March 16. (tirto.id)

The 50+10 mL pack shows a workable model. A value set made for one channel keeps club pricing from undercutting prestige shelf prices. Club and off-price buyers talking to Korean derma brands should ask for an exclusive bundle instead of the standard SKU.

3. C-beauty exports reach about 39% of Korea's

According to the South China Morning Post, China exported about US$3.99bn of cosmetics in January–November 2025 (+8.7%). Korea exported a record US$10.3bn (+11.8%) over the same months. (SCMP)

  • China's cosmetics imports fell 3.4% to US$11.63bn over those months, a sign that local brands are taking share at home.

Korea still exports more than 2.5 times as much. But Chinese brands that grow strong at home are now looking abroad, and they will land in the same mid- and entry-price tiers where Korean indie brands sell. Buyers will have more options in that tier. Compare suppliers on actives and clinical data, not only on landed cost.

4. Artbox looks for U.S. stores with CBRE

CBRE has reportedly been named exclusive U.S. leasing agent for Artbox, a Korean lifestyle retailer with 222 stores at home. The appointment was announced October 15. (CBRE) Artbox's first direct U.S. activation was a pop-up on Melrose Avenue in Los Angeles from October 3 to 11. A permanent store is planned. (FashionUnited)

Korean variety stores could become a U.S. shelf for indie K-beauty brands that are not ready for Ulta or Target. U.S. specialty retailers should watch where Artbox signs leases, because it will compete for the same K-culture shoppers.
