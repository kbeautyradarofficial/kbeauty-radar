1. US$11.1bn in nine months, with no sign of a stall yet

Provisional MFDS data show Korea exported US$11.1bn of cosmetics in Jan–Sep 2026, up 31.1% YoY. MFDS says that is the highest nine-month total on record (MFDS via KDI Economic Information Center).

Q3 on its own came to US$4.17bn, up 7.5% on Q2's US$3.88bn. Next to +31% YoY, that quarter-on-quarter figure looks weak. But one number compares with the previous quarter and the other with last year. Cumulative growth was +27% at H1, on US$7.0bn (CNC News). By September it had reached +31.1%, which means year-on-year growth sped up in Q3.

  • For buyers: Korean supply is still running hot going into Q4. Lock in holiday and Q1 allocations on hero SKUs early.

2. The US is the No.1 market: US$2.35bn, a 21.1% share

Shipments to the US reached US$2.35bn in Jan–Sep (+40.6% YoY). China followed at US$1.58bn and Japan at US$0.89bn (MFDS via KDI). The US share was 20.7% at H1, so its weight is still growing (CNC News).

CNC News reports that SMEs and mid-sized firms made up 91.1% of Korean cosmetics exports in 2025 (CNC News). That means many of your suppliers are small teams paying for MoCRA registration, labelling and tariffs out of thin margins.

  • For buyers: add MoCRA facility registration and product listing status to the onboarding checklist for every new K-beauty supplier.

3. Skincare is US$8.72bn; body cleansing grew 30.1%

Basic skincare accounted for US$8.72bn of Jan–Sep exports, US$2.71bn more than a year earlier. Body-cleansing products rose 30.1%, and makeup slipped slightly (MFDS via KDI).

Skincare is roughly 79% of exports, and body care is the adjacent category growing fastest. The growth in Korean supply is in skincare and body care, not colour cosmetics.

  • For retailers: check your K-beauty body care assortment. If the shelf is mostly face care and colour, it is missing the part of Korean supply that is growing.

4. Olive Young US: after two LA-area stores, a target of five in the West

CJ Olive Young reportedly opened an 803㎡ standalone store in Pasadena on 29 May, together with a US-only online store (CNC News / CJ Newsroom). A 250㎡ store followed at Westfield Century City on 13 June. Its skincare shelving is 1.5x that of a standard Korean store, and the opening-day queue was reported at more than 100m (Digital Daily).

The plan is to run five stores in the Western US, centred on California, by H1 2027, and then expand to the East and South-Central regions (Digital Daily). Its Global Mall is used by shoppers in 150 countries, with most sales coming from North America. No sales figure has been disclosed (Newsis).

  • For US retailers: expect more competition for exclusives on trending Korean brands. Agree launch windows with the brands you want before Olive Young adds more stores.

5. Charmzone: revenue down 86%, and a food company is the bidder

Dongwon Home Food has reportedly been named final preferred bidder for Charmzone, which is in court receivership. The price is reported at 수백억원, which means tens of billions of won. The deal is not final until the Seoul Bankruptcy Court process is complete (CEO Score Daily).

According to IB Tomato, Charmzone's revenue fell from ₩128.8bn in 2022 to ₩18.1bn in 2025. It made an operating loss every year from 2020 to 2025, and its equity was –₩35.2bn at end-2025 (IB Tomato). The buyer is paying for brand recognition and distribution, not earnings.

  • For distributors: if you hold Charmzone contracts, check how assignment and supply continuity will work under receivership before you reorder.
